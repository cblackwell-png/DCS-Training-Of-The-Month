What you'll learn

  • What psychological safety is — and what it isn't
  • Why the strongest teams surface problems instead of hiding them
  • Everyday habits that make it safe for others to speak up
  • How to respond when someone disagrees, takes a risk, or admits a mistake
  • Small, repeatable moves that build trust across a team over time

Why this matters at DC SCORES

Our work with poet-athletes depends on people speaking up — flagging a safety concern, admitting a mistake, offering a half-formed idea. Psychological safety is what makes that possible. It's the ground every other part of our culture is built on, and it's something all of us create for each other, every day.

Why this training, why now

The context behind making psychological safety our shared foundation this month.

"Every idea a poet-athlete ever shares with us starts with them believing it's safe to raise a hand. We owe each other that same safety. It isn't soft — it's the strongest thing a team can build."

#1

In Google's two-year Project Aristotle study of its own teams, psychological safety was the single strongest predictor of a team's performance — above talent, resources, or tenure.

Coined at Harvard

Harvard's Amy Edmondson — who teaches this month's course — defined psychological safety and showed it's what lets teams learn, adapt, and speak up.

Safe to say "I was wrong"

In Edmondson's research, the best teams reported more mistakes — not because they made more, but because they felt safe enough to name them and fix them fast.

Sources: Google re:Work, Project Aristotle; Amy C. Edmondson, The Fearless Organization (2019) and her foundational research on team psychological safety.

Common questions

Quick answers to the things people ask most. Still stuck? Reach out to People & Culture.

I've completed this training, or one like it, before — do I need to do it again?
Yes. This is the first time we're making it a shared requirement for everyone on staff, so we're all completing it together now — even if you've taken something similar in the past. Going forward it becomes an annual refresh, keeping the whole team on the same, up-to-date footing.
How do I log in to LinkedIn Learning?
Use your dcscores.org account. Click "Start on LinkedIn Learning" above and sign in with your work email — you'll be routed through single sign-on. If you hit a wall, email People & Culture and we'll get you access.
I need an accommodation to complete this training.
We've got you. Whether it's captions, extra time, a different format, or anything else, contact People & Culture and we'll arrange an alternative that works for you — no need to share details you're not comfortable sharing.
What if I can't finish by the deadline?
Let us know before the date if you can. Deadlines keep us all accountable, but life happens — email People & Culture and we'll sort out a plan.
